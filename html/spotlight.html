The Engineer Behind the Code

On building systems she actually wants to use, finding the love of her life in AI, and why she can tell the difference between an ML engineer and someone who just calls an API

Interview by The Portfolio Magazine
July 2026

Susana Loureiro doesn't build things to fill a portfolio. She builds them because they fix a real problem — usually one of hers. Precise about what she is and what she isn't, she is not particularly interested in pretending otherwise.

How would you describe the kind of engineer you are?
The kind that doesn't do something just because. If I build something, it's because it matters — not because it looks good on a CV, not because it makes money. It has to fix a real problem or solve something I actually care about.
Your projects are very specific — they come from a real place. Where do the ideas come from?
From things I like or problems I have. The people-counting project was a company need, but the fashion projects came from me. I always wished I had a personal stylist — someone to just tell me what to wear when I'm not in the mood to figure it out, which is almost all the time. So I built one. Others come from wanting to experiment with things I'm drawn to. For example, I built a music generation project for exactly the same reason.
I always wished I had a personal stylist. So I built one.
At what point did you know ML and AI was yours?
I'm not sure there was a point. I just always knew. I got into the area through AWS certifications — I chose the AI ones because something about that direction appealed to me, even if I couldn't fully explain it at the time. I followed the instinct and never questioned it.
You go deep — building transformers from scratch rather than just using pre-built models. Why?
Because I want to understand how things work under the hood. Calling an API doesn't feel like engineering to me. If I don't know what's happening underneath, I'm not building anything — I'm just using someone else's work.
I like building systems. I love building intelligent ones.
You talk about building intelligent systems, not just automated ones. What's the difference to you?
I believe machines can be our little assistants in the same way dogs are a human's best friend — helping us in ways that are more technical, more precise, but just as useful. An automated system just executes. An intelligent system understands. That distinction is why I do this.
Where do you want to be in a few years?
Full ML/AI engineer. And building towards making her. an actual product rather than a spare project. That's the long-term goal — being the CEO of something that started because I had a problem and decided to solve it. I'm not in a rush, but I'm not not thinking about it either.

Susana Loureiro, Porto. July 2026.

Quick Facts

Machine Learning & AI Engineer based in Porto, Portugal. BSc in Informatics Engineering from ISEP. Fluent in Portuguese and English.
