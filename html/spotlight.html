She Builds
What She
Wishes Existed.
On building intelligent systems from scratch, finishing a degree during one of the hardest years of her life, and why calling an API is not, in her view, engineering.
Susana Loureiro has a rule — more of a reflex, really: if she doesn't understand how something works under the hood, she doesn't feel like she's built anything at all. At 22, she's already shipping projects that solve problems she actually has.
By The Portfolio Magazine · Sept 2026

It's the second week of September, and Susana Loureiro is at her apartment in Porto, the one she lives and works in. In two days she starts a master's in AI Engineering, alongside a full-time job. She's 22, and she's describing all of this the way most people describe a dentist appointment. Like it's just what's happening next.

There's something immediately precise about her. Not cold, but precise. She thinks before she speaks, and when she speaks, she means it. She's the kind of person who will spend five months rebuilding the same model architecture because the first four versions weren't good enough, not because someone told her to, but because she couldn't live with anything less. That instinct, the refusal to borrow what she could build, is the thread that runs through everything she does. It also explains why, when you ask her what she's working on, the answer is never one thing.

Her flagship project, her., is a personal AI stylist. Not the kind that recommends a trending item because an algorithm noticed you clicked on beige. The kind that actually learns your wardrobe, understands what goes together, and gives you an answer that feels like it came from someone who knows you. Underneath it lives stylist.: a compatibility model she trained from scratch on tens of thousands of outfits, using a Siamese network with triplet loss and hard negative mining. She rebuilt the architecture five times before she was satisfied.

"Calling someone else's API and putting my name on the result doesn't feel like engineering to me. If I don't understand what's happening underneath, I haven't built anything."

She's not performing modesty either. The accuracy sits at 70–75%, and she'll tell you exactly why: the ceiling is in the training data, not the architecture. She already knows how to fix it. She already has a plan for that too.

Alongside her., she built core., her own agentic AI assistant, with access to her schedule, her tasks, her finances. She tried every planner app that existed first. Used each one for a day, then abandoned it. "None of them felt mine," she says simply. So she built one that did, with a C# backend, a Python agent, and a frontend in Angular. The pattern is not coincidence. It's character. Every tool she reaches for that doesn't fit becomes something she builds herself.

She completed three AWS certifications: AI Practitioner, ML Engineer Associate, Solutions Architect Associate. All of them in four months, on her own initiative, while finishing her degree and building all of this. When asked who she was trying to prove something to, she doesn't hesitate. "Myself. That's it." And then, almost as an afterthought: "I followed the instinct toward AI before I could fully explain it. The certifications were just the proof that I was right."

The master's she's starting is the AI Engineering one at ISEP. She chose it deliberately over another offer in Data Engineering, because she knew which direction she was going and she doesn't waste time on the scenic route. The degree, she's clear, is not the destination. It's the credential she picks up on the way to something bigger.

That something bigger is her. as a real company. As a product in market, paying customers, built on technology she understands completely, inside and out. She says 2030 like it's already decided. And sitting across from her, in the September quiet of an apartment that still smells like a fresh start, it's hard to argue.

Before she has to go, there's one last question. Not about the projects or the certifications or the master's. About her. Who does she want to be, when all of this is done?

"Confident. A genius. An engineer. I already know I'm the last one. The rest I'm working on."
